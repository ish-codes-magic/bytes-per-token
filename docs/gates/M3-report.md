# Gate Report: M3, quantization from scratch

> **Gate decision:** pending. The explain-it-back questions below are for later study; M4 can start on approval.

## What was built

- **Reference quantizers** in plain PyTorch ([`src/fastserve/quant/`](../../src/fastserve/quant/)), each
  unit-tested:
  - **`rtn.py`:** integer grids at any width and granularity, symmetric/asymmetric, with the textbook or
    compressed-tensors' full-range symmetric grid, and exact storage accounting.
  - **`formats.py`:** FP8 E4M3/E5M2 rounding (bit-exact against PyTorch's float8 casts) and NF4, built from
    normal quantiles (matching bitsandbytes' table).
  - **`gptq.py`:** GPTQ with dampening, act-order and static groups. It equals a naive Optimal-Brain-Surgeon loop
    at every block size tested.
  - **`awq.py`:** AWQ with duo or paper scaling, a parent-module loss, and optional clipping.
  - **`rotation.py`:** norm folding plus residual-stream rotation by a random Hadamard matrix; outputs unchanged.
  - **`w8a8.py`:** FP8/INT8 weights and activations, with static, dynamic or per-token scales, and SmoothQuant.
  - **`model.py`:** calibration hooks and the layer-by-layer drivers.
- **Library validation:** llm-compressor 0.14.0 in its own image quantizes the same model with the same
  calibration tokens ([`experiments/m3_library.py`](../../src/fastserve/experiments/m3_library.py)). Its defaults
  were read from the installed source first.
- **A campaign of every configuration** in [the config](../../benchmarks/configs/m3_quant.yaml), one container per task,
  plus an outlier atlas, a sensitivity scan, a GPTQ trace, a calibration study, a model-size comparison and a
  follow-up rotation diagnosis.
- **A compute log from Modal's billing API** ([`scripts/compute_log.py`](../../scripts/compute_log.py)).
- **Docs:** the [M3 learning doc](../learning/M3-quant-reference.md) (with the GPTQ derivation and a worked
  example generated by the code), predictions committed before any real model was quantized (`16766ba`).

Every gap is explained in the [learning doc](../learning/M3-quant-reference.md#explaining-every-gap). In short:
- **Round-to-nearest is harsher than predicted** because of massive activations. A weight column that reads a
  1,000× channel turns ordinary rounding error into a huge output error. That's also why AWQ beats GPTQ here.
- **Rotation hurt RTN because of folding:** the RMSNorm γ (up to 61× uneven) becomes outlier weight columns.
  With GPTQ, rotation gives the best INT4 result.
- **Calibration size barely matters; its domain does.**

## Surprises and dead ends

- **Our first AWQ wasn't the standard AWQ.** Reading llm-compressor's installed source showed three
  differences: duo scaling, a loss on the parent module, and no clipping. The library also uses a different
  INT4 grid. Our code now offers both versions, and the comparison matches settings.
- **A wrong test:** I expected per-token FP8 activation scales to beat per-tensor ones, as for INT8. They
  don't: a logarithmic grid has scale-free relative precision. The test now states the contrast.
- **The first worked example showed nothing:** GPTQ tied RTN because no compensation crossed a rounding
  boundary. A small search found an example where it does.
- **Rotation made things worse,** until a diagnosis run separated folding, rotating and rounding.
- **INT8 improved the 1.7B model's perplexity** while changing its predictions as much as the 0.6B's: a reminder
  of why KL is the headline metric.
- **Missing since M0:** `results/compute_log.csv`, planned but never written. It now comes from Modal's billing API.
- **Deviations from AGENTS.md:**
  - W8A8 lives in `w8a8.py` (FP8 and INT8 together) rather than `fp8.py`.
  - Rotation covers the residual stream (QuaRot's R1), not the online Hadamard before down_proj.
  - The quality metric is KL and perplexity. Task scores wait for M4, where quantized checkpoints run in vLLM.

## What you should now understand

- Grids: scale, zero-point, granularity, their storage cost, and why the textbook INT4 grid wastes a code.
- Why FP8's logarithmic grid tolerates outliers and NF4's quantile grid fits bell-shaped weights.
- GPTQ from its objective: the Hessian, the OBS update, the Cholesky trick, lazy batches, act-order.
- AWQ and SmoothQuant as two uses of one identity, x Wᵀ = (x/s)(W·s)ᵀ.
- Why a rotation preserves the function, what it needs (folded norms, an untied head), and what that can cost.
- Massive activations: where they come from and why they make quantization of small models hard.

## Proposed next steps: M4, quantization in production

- **Checkpoints:** produce FP8 (W8A8) and INT4 (GPTQ, AWQ) checkpoints with llm-compressor, serve them in vLLM
  on the L4, and compare against our reference versions (closing the loop with M3).
- **Speed:** a batch-size sweep of decode throughput and TPOT per format, looking for the **crossover**.
  Prediction from M2: INT4 should win at small batch, where the weights are most of the bytes, and fade at
  large batch, where the KV cache dominates.
- **Quality in the served models:** lm-eval task scores, which M3 didn't run.
- **The first waterfall bar:** $ per 1M tokens, BF16 → FP8 (→ INT4).
